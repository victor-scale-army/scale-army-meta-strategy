What Alex wants to happen, step by step

  1. Someone fills out our lead form.
  2. We wait about an hour before telling Meta about it.
  3. In that hour, we find out two things: how big their company is, and whether they actually booked a call.
  4. We tell Meta about the lead — but now attach a score: bigger companies score higher, up to a sweet spot (~50–200 employees), then score lower again for huge enterprises. Booking a call scores much higher than not booking.
  5. We tell Meta to spend our budget chasing the highest total score, not just the most leads.

Which parts of this are normal, and which aren't

Standard — not new

  • Nothing on the site gets removed — the Pixel keeps firing exactly as it does today
  • CAPI is a second, separate channel, sent from HubSpot's server — not GTM (GTM only runs in the browser, so it can't act on something that happens in the CRM after the visit is over)
  • Matching doesn't rely on one click ID surviving — the lead's hashed email and phone go along with it, and Meta matches on whichever identifiers it has
  • Email is one of the strongest signals precisely because it doesn't depend on a cookie or browser session still being alive
  • Server-side CAPI matched by contact details is a well-documented pattern any CRM-driven business uses to advertise on Meta

Actually new — fair to be skeptical of

  • Collapsing the whole signal into one artificially delayed event
  • ...carrying a hand-built score we invented ourselves
  • ...instead of sending each real pipeline event (Lead, MQL, MB, MH...) as it naturally happens
  • This is the layer worth stress-testing before committing — not the tracking plumbing underneath it

Feasibility check

Can HubSpot actually do this? Partly — not the part that matters

HubSpot does have a native Meta Conversions API integration. But neither of its two native paths can deliver what this plan specifically needs: one event, delayed, carrying a value that changes per lead.

What HubSpot native CAPI does do

  • Sends conversion events to Meta server-side, not just via the browser pixel
  • Sends both server and browser events so Meta can deduplicate them
  • Matches on email, phone, click ID, IP and user agent — email recommended for better identity match
  • Triggers on: HubSpot form submissions, lifecycle stage changes, page views
  • Available on Marketing Hub Starter (5 events) / Professional (50) / Enterprise (100)

Where it breaks for this plan

  • Form-submission events: one fixed value, and that's it. You configure the event and "specify a monetary value for your event" — a single number applied to every lead that triggers it. There is no way to vary it per lead, so the 14-value table can't be expressed. They also fire immediately, with no delay.
  • Lifecycle-stage events: no value field at all. Nothing to weight with.
  • And the enrichment isn't reliable enough to score on. Per Elena, in August we could not find company size for 96 of 283 leads — only 187 resolved. That's a 34% miss rate on the single input the whole scoring model depends on.

Neither native option can carry a per-lead value. The weighted-score model can't be built on HubSpot's native integration.

The alternative

The simple path — no custom code, no new tools

There is a native way to get most of what this plan wants. We create a qualified-lead lifecycle stage (like the MQL we already have), let a HubSpot workflow move leads into it once enrichment confirms the company is big enough, and sync that lifecycle stage to Meta as the conversion event.

Lead submits form → Enrichment runs
company size resolves → Workflow moves lead to
"Qualified Lead" stage → HubSpot syncs it to Meta
native CAPI — no code

Why this is attractive

  • Fully native. No custom code, no webhook, no extra HubSpot tier to buy, no third-party connector. Roughly 1–2 hours of setup.
  • The delay problem disappears. We don't need an artificial 60-minute wait — the event fires exactly when qualification actually resolves, whenever that is.
  • Quality is baked into the event itself rather than into a fragile score. A "Qualified Lead" event only fires for leads that passed the size check.

What we give up — and the catch

  • No weighting at all. Lifecycle-stage events carry no value field, so every qualified lead counts the same. No size bands, no hump curve, no booked-vs-not multiplier.
  • The catch is volume. By the proposal's own numbers, a size-qualified event runs ~45/week — below the ~50/week learning threshold, while the raw Lead event runs ~70/week and clears it. And the 34% enrichment miss rate pushes it lower still: a lead with no company size can't enter the qualified stage at all.

The real constraint isn't the money — it's the readable window

Exiting the learning phase takes roughly a week of hitting the ~50 conversions/week threshold. Everything before that is not a result we can judge. Subtract that week from the "weeks new structure runs" column above and you get the actual amount of readable data September buys us:

  • 7-day setup → ~2.3 weeks of post-learning data
  • 10-day setup → ~1.9 weeks
  • 21-day setup → ~0.3 weeks — effectively nothing

What that means for the decision

  • At a 3-week setup we would spend the full $125K and end September with ~2 days of judgeable data on the new structure.
  • The verdict lands in October either way — no setup timeline produces a September answer.
  • If setup realistically runs past ~10 days, the honest framing is that September is a setup month, not a test month — and we should plan the budget and expectations around that rather than around a result.
  • This argues for nailing down the setup timeline before committing to the switch, not after.

Side by side

Pros, cons, risks

A · Validated

  • + Already proven to work — not perfectly, but it works
  • + ABO spreads risk across creatives (portfolio-safe)
  • − Doesn't encode buyer persona/angle/quality at all
  • − Many small campaigns already fragment volume account-wide
  • − Judges performance ad-by-ad, which both other models agree is unreliable

B · Andromeda test

  • + Matches documented Meta mechanics (creative = targeting)
  • + Concrete persona/angle bank already built to fund it
  • − Directly fragments volume below the learning threshold
  • − Live evidence: $7,982 spent, 0 Meetings Held or SQLs so far
  • − Heavy creative load per concept (10–20+ ads to validate one)

C · Evergreen (proposed)

  • + Only model built from our actual weekly volume numbers
  • + Encodes lead quality directly (size + booking → value)
  • − Real martech lift (CAPI, enrichment, value table) before launch
  • − Risk of offer cannibalization inside one shared ad set
  • − Contradicts the Andromeda video's own warning: mixing many different personas/roles in one ad set can confuse Meta's audience-finding — unproven either way at our volume

Recommended steps

Step by step

Weeks 1–3

Signal

  1. Check the Page ad-limit band. Meta caps active/in-review ads per Page based on its highest-spending month (250 ads under $100K/mo, 1,000 under $1M) — that's our real ceiling on active creative, not any ad-set number.
  2. Wire domain enrichment into step 1 of the form. Resolve company size from the email domain the moment someone submits — before they ever reach the booking page. Treat unresolved domains as pass-through, not a block: enrichment coverage is weakest on exactly the small/local companies we most want.
  3. Get HubSpot → Meta CAPI live. Server-side, deduplicated against the on-site Pixel, so Meta learns what happened after the form fill, not just the click.
  4. Fire the full 6-event ladder for reporting. Lead, Size-Qualified Lead, Meeting Booked, Meeting Held, Opportunity, Closed Won — all six flow to Meta for reporting, even though we'll only optimize bidding on one of them.
  5. Build the conversion-value table. Company-size band × booked-or-not → a score. Placeholder values are fine to launch with — the shape of the curve (where it peaks) matters far more than precise dollar figures.
  6. Set the Lead event to fire on a ~60-minute delay, with the booking outcome attached. We wait to observe the real outcome instead of guessing — this costs nothing since Meta's click attribution window is 7 days.
  7. Gate the booking calendar in the scheduling tool. The form asks company size; answering 10 or fewer employees blocks the booking. Protects AE time and makes "Meeting Booked" qualified by construction.
Weeks 3–5

Structure

  1. Collapse into the single Evergreen campaign/ad set. Consolidate the account down to essentially one campaign, one ad set holding every established offer.
  2. Switch bid strategy to maximize conversion value on Lead. Meta chases the total size/booking-weighted score, not just the most email addresses.
  3. Migrate existing creative in — including any winning creative from the 94/95 Andromeda test, once its current learning window is judged.
  4. Establish and backfill the naming convention (Offer/Buyer/Pain/Angle/Proof/Format/Awareness/Destination). Tag every creative across these dimensions in the ad name itself, so persona/angle thinking still lives somewhere — just in the taxonomy, not the account structure.
  5. Set a weekly batch cadence for new creative (~10/week, not monthly dumps) — staggered cohorts get cleaner reads than dumping everything in at once.
Weeks 5–10

Stabilize

  1. Let it run without structural intervention. Resist the urge to tinker with the account once it's live.
  2. Weekly monitoring only — no ad-level kills. Don't pause ads for low delivery or a bad attributed CPA; that's the system's verdict, not a mistake to fix.
  3. Confirm lead volume holds above 50/week post-switch. This is the number the entire model depends on — if it drops below threshold, the consolidation stops working.
  4. Backfill-enrich historical HubSpot leads to replace the placeholder value table with real booking/close rates by company-size band.
  5. Establish the real cost-per-booked-meeting baseline that future tests will be judged against.
Week 10+

Explore

  1. Pre-register the first in-funnel offer test. Before spending a dollar: write down the hypothesis, required creative volume, landing page, duration, and kill/graduate thresholds.
  2. Build 8–12 meaningfully different concepts and a matched, uniquely tagged landing page — enough distinct concepts for a statistically readable result, and an LP that continues the conversation the ad started.
  3. Launch into evergreen and read it in HubSpot by landing page — not in Ads Manager. A new offer producing cheap, low-value leads will still look busy in Meta while contributing nothing.
