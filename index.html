What Alex wants to happen, step by step

  1. Someone fills out our lead form.
  2. We wait about an hour before telling Meta about it.
  3. In that hour, we find out two things: how big their company is, and whether they actually booked a call.
  4. We tell Meta about the lead — but now attach a score: bigger companies score higher, up to a sweet spot (~50–200 employees), then score lower again for huge enterprises. Booking a call scores much higher than not booking.
  5. We tell Meta to spend our budget chasing the highest total score, not just the most leads.

What we'd actually need to do

  1. Verify what's really connected today — a real server-side integration (HubSpot's native Meta Ads tool), or just a browser-side Pixel via GTM. They are not the same thing, and only the first can carry data that resolves after the visit.
  2. If it isn't server-side yet, connect it — configured to match leads by email/phone, not only a click ID.
  3. Keep the on-site Pixel running as-is — this doesn't replace it, it adds a second, more reliable channel alongside it.
  4. Get a real answer on the enrichment tool's failure rate — it already fails (runs out of credits) — before betting this on it. "Turn it on" isn't enough; we need to know what happens when it fails.
  5. Gate the booking calendar on that (verified) enrichment result.
  6. Build the value table — the size × booked → score lookup all of this feeds.
How the event actually reaches Meta. This part sounds unusual, so here's the mechanism plainly.
Pixel fires in browser
real-time, while they're on the site — unchanged + HubSpot sends CAPI event later
server-side, whenever size/booking is known → Meta matches both
via email, phone, IP — not only a click ID

Standard — not new

  • Nothing on the site gets removed — the Pixel keeps firing exactly as it does today
  • CAPI is a second, separate channel, sent from HubSpot's server — not GTM (GTM only runs in the browser, so it can't act on something that happens in the CRM after the visit is over)
  • Matching doesn't rely on one click ID surviving — the lead's hashed email and phone go along with it, and Meta matches on whichever identifiers it has
  • Email is one of the strongest signals precisely because it doesn't depend on a cookie or browser session still being alive
  • Server-side CAPI matched by contact details is a well-documented pattern any CRM-driven business uses to advertise on Meta

Actually new — fair to be skeptical of

  • Collapsing the whole signal into one artificially delayed event
  • ...carrying a hand-built score we invented ourselves
  • ...instead of sending each real pipeline event (Lead, MQL, MB, MH...) as it naturally happens
  • This is the layer worth stress-testing before committing — not the tracking plumbing underneath it

Side by side

Pros, cons, risks

A · Validated

  • + Proven across ~98 campaigns; team already fluent in it
  • + ABO spreads risk across creatives (portfolio-safe)
  • − Doesn't encode buyer persona/angle/quality at all
  • − Many small campaigns already fragment volume account-wide
  • − Judges performance ad-by-ad, which both other models agree is unreliable

B · Andromeda test

  • + Matches documented Meta mechanics (creative = targeting)
  • + Concrete persona/angle bank already built to fund it
  • − Directly fragments volume below the learning threshold
  • − Live evidence: $7,982 spent, 0 Meetings Held or SQLs so far
  • − Heavy creative load per concept (10–20+ ads to validate one)

C · Evergreen (proposed)

  • + Only model built from our actual weekly volume numbers
  • + Encodes lead quality directly (size + booking → value)
  • + Comes with a full rollout plan, do-not list, kill/graduate rules
  • − Real martech lift (CAPI, enrichment, value table) before launch
  • − Conflicts with the just-announced scheduling tool (see next)
  • − Risk of offer cannibalization inside one shared ad set

Needs resolving before anything ships

Two decisions from the same person, in conflict

The scheduling tool (this week)

New internal booking form asks the lead to self-report company size. Under 10 employees → disqualified via GTM.

§2.2a of Alex's own doc

"Do not use a self-reported company size field. Anyone motivated to book will select whatever band gets them through... Enrich off the domain instead — more accurate, invisible to the user, available in the same session."

This isn't a nuance to quietly resolve on our own. Building the scheduling tool on self-report now, only to redo it for domain enrichment next month, is wasted engineering effort either way. Needs a direct answer from Alex.

Recommendation

Move toward Evergreen — but sequenced, not switched overnight

Do this, in this order

The account evidence already backs Alex's math: 8 ad sets, $7,982 spent, 9 conversions, zero exits from learning. That's not a future risk — it's this month. Of the three models, Evergreen is the only one designed around our actual weekly volume rather than general best practice, and it comes with the most complete operating plan (rollout, do-not list, kill/graduate rules).

But the doc itself is explicit: no structural change before the signal work (CAPI, enrichment, value curve) is live. Collapsing to one ad set today, on top of a shallow event with no value model, would just consolidate spend behind bad signal faster.

Don't kill 94/95 mid-air either — let their current learning window finish or fold their winning creative into evergreen once it's live, per the doc's own graduation logic. The persona/angle map isn't wasted — it becomes the creative taxonomy and naming layer, not the ad set structure.
